Synchronization

Synchronization of coupled oscillators: the Kuramoto model on networks, and its higher-order versions on simplicial complexes, where oscillators live on edges and triangles too.

Every oscillator has a phase that turns at its own natural frequency and is pulled towards the phases it is coupled with, with strength sigma. Weakly coupled, the phases drift independently; strongly coupled, they lock together. The order parameter \(R = |\frac{1}{n} \sum_j e^{i \theta_j}|\) measures this, from 0 (incoherent) to 1 (synchronized), and its curve against sigma shows the synchronization transition, continuous or explosive (abrupt, with hysteresis).

  • kuramoto_model(): phases on the nodes of a network (Kuramoto, 1975; on networks, Arenas, Díaz-Guilera, Kurths, Moreno and Zhou, 2008): \(\dot\theta_i = \omega_i + \sigma \sum_j A_{ji} \sin(\theta_j - \theta_i)\), with weights and directions.

  • simplicial_kuramoto_model(): phases on the \(k\)-simplices of a simplicial complex, coupled through the boundary matrices (Millán, Torres and Bianconi, 2020), with the explosive variant; \(k = 1\) puts the oscillators on the edges of a complex or of a network.

  • node_link_kuramoto_model(): phases on the nodes and on the edges at once, each coupling modulated by the order of the other (Ghorbanchian, Restrepo, Torres and Bianconi, 2021), whose explosive transition comes from the coupling of the two topological signals.

The dynamics are integrated with the classical Runge-Kutta method (fixed step dt), in double precision, on the device of the network, and order parameters are averaged over time after a transient. A value of sigma or a sequence of them can be given, with independent runs (frequencies and initial phases drawn per run, shared by all sigma values). Without adiabatic, all pairs of sigma and run are integrated at once, as the columns of one matrix: a whole transition curve at the cost of a single simulation of many columns, which suits a GPU. With adiabatic, the values of sigma are followed in the given order, each starting from the final phases of the previous one: a forward sweep followed by a backward one shows the hysteresis loop of an explosive transition.

The coupling uses the boundary matrices of boundary_matrix(): \(B_1\) maps edges to nodes, \(B_2\) triangles to edges, and simplices have the reference orientation of their nodes (see set_simplex_attribute()). A plain network is a complex without triangles: its edges, taken undirected, unweighted and without self-loops or repetitions, in the order of their node pairs (internal indices), are its 1-simplices.

import graphoskop as gsk
import numpy as np

net = gsk.load_network("edges.tsv")
curve = gsk.kuramoto_model(net, np.linspace(0, 1, 21), runs = 10, seed = 0)   # all at once
curve.groupby("sigma")["order"].mean()                                         # the transition

sc = gsk.simplicial_configuration_model(degrees = [6] * 200, dimensions = {2: 400}, seed = 1)
up = np.linspace(0.05, 4, 16)
edges = gsk.simplicial_kuramoto_model(sc, up, k = 1, omega = (2., 1.), seed = 0)   # oscillators on the edges
loop = gsk.node_link_kuramoto_model(sc, np.r_[up, up[::-1]], model = "nlt", omega = (2., 1.), link_omega = (2., 1.), adiabatic = True, seed = 1)
forward, backward = loop["order_nodes"][:16], loop["order_nodes"][16:]         # explosive: a hysteresis loop

Higher-order Kuramoto models

graphoskop.node_link_kuramoto_model(net, sigma, model='nl', omega=(0.0, 1.0), link_omega=(0.0, 1.0), theta=None, phi=None, component=0, runs=1, adiabatic=False, time=100.0, dt=0.01, transient=None, seed=None, phases=False, progress=None)

Coupled Kuramoto dynamics of node and link signals (Ghorbanchian, Restrepo, Torres and Bianconi, 2021): phases \(\theta\) on the nodes and \(\phi\) on the edges of a network or simplicial complex, each coupled through the boundary matrices and modulated by the order of the other,

\[\begin{split}\dot\theta &= \omega - \sigma R_1^{down} B_1 \sin(B_1^T \theta), \\ \dot\phi &= \tilde\omega - \sigma R_0 (R_1^{up})^{m - 1} B_1^T \sin(B_1 \phi) - \sigma (R_1^{down})^{m - 1} B_2 \sin(B_2^T \phi),\end{split}\]

with the order parameters \(R_0\) of the node phases (order_nodes), \(R_1^{down}\) of the projection of the link phases on the nodes, \(B_1 \phi\) (order_down), and \(R_1^{up}\) of their projection on the triangles, \(B_2^T \phi\) (order_up). Model "nl" (\(m = 1\)) couples nodes and links; model "nlt" (\(m = 2\)) also couples the triangles, and needs them. The coupling of the two signals makes the transition explosive, with hysteresis (follow it with adiabatic). On a plain network there are no triangles: the last term is absent and order_up is NaN. See the module description for sigma, runs and adiabatic.

Simplicial complexes: supported; hypergraphs: not supported (see Higher-order networks).

Parameters:

  • net – A Network or a simplicial complex.

  • sigma – Coupling strength, or sequence of them.

  • model – "nl" (nodes and links) or "nlt" (nodes, links and triangles).

  • omega – Natural frequencies of the nodes, (mean, sd) of a normal distribution or values (see kuramoto_model()).

  • link_omega – Natural frequencies of the edges, as omega, over the edges in the order of simplices() (or the sorted edges of a network).

  • theta – Initial node phases, None for uniform ones.

  • phi – Initial edge phases, None for uniform ones.

  • component – Index of the component (0 is the largest).

  • runs – Number of independent runs.

  • adiabatic – Whether to follow the values of sigma in order (see kuramoto_model()).

  • time – Integration time of each value of sigma.

  • dt – Time step.

  • transient – Time discarded before averaging, or None for half of time.

  • seed – Seed of the random numbers, or None for a random seed.

  • phases – Whether to also return the final phases.

  • progress – Whether to show a progress bar over the time steps, or None for the global setting (see Progress bars).

Returns:

pandas.DataFrame with one row per value of sigma and run: columns sigma, run, order_nodes, order_down and order_up; with phases, also the final node and edge phases, tensors [n, len(sigma), runs] and [edges, len(sigma), runs].

Raises:

ValueError – If net is a hypergraph or has no edges, model is unknown or "nlt" without triangles, a vector has the wrong shape, or the times are inconsistent.

Example:

import graphoskop as gsk
import numpy as np

net = gsk.load_network("edges.tsv")
up = np.linspace(0, 5, 26)
loop = gsk.node_link_kuramoto_model(net, np.r_[up, up[::-1]], omega = (2., 1.), link_omega = (2., 1.), adiabatic = True, seed = 0)
graphoskop.simplicial_kuramoto_model(net, sigma, k=1, explosive=False, omega=(0.0, 1.0), theta=None, component=0, runs=1, adiabatic=False, time=100.0, dt=0.01, transient=None, seed=None, phases=False, progress=None)

Higher-order Kuramoto model of Millán, Torres and Bianconi (2020): oscillators on the \(k\)-simplices of a simplicial complex (on its edges for \(k = 1\): a topological signal), each coupled with the simplices it shares a face or a coface with,

\[\dot\theta = \omega - \sigma B_{k + 1} \sin(B_{k + 1}^T \theta) - \sigma B_k^T \sin(B_k \theta).\]

The phases project on the \((k - 1)\)-simplices, \(\theta^- = B_k \theta\) (a discrete divergence), and on the \((k + 1)\)-simplices, \(\theta^+ = B_{k + 1}^T \theta\) (a discrete curl), whose dynamics decouple; their order parameters order_down and order_up (\(R^-\) and \(R^+\)) synchronize, while the phases themselves need not (order). With explosive, each coupling is modulated by the order of the other projection, \(\sigma R^-\) on the first term and \(\sigma R^+\) on the second, which makes the transition discontinuous, with hysteresis (follow it with adiabatic).

For \(k = 0\) this is the Kuramoto model of the 1-skeleton (kuramoto_model()). On a plain network, \(k\) is 0 or 1, and edges have no triangles: the second term only. The order parameter of a projection with no simplex (order_down for \(k = 0\), order_up without \((k + 1)\)-simplices) is NaN. Phases are over simplices() (or the sorted edges of a network), in reference orientation. See the module description for sigma, runs and adiabatic.

Simplicial complexes: supported; hypergraphs: not supported (see Higher-order networks).

Parameters:

  • net – A simplicial complex or a Network.

  • sigma – Coupling strength, or sequence of them.

  • k – Dimension of the simplices carrying the oscillators.

  • explosive – Whether to use the explosive (adaptive) coupling.

  • omega – Natural frequencies, (mean, sd) of a normal distribution or values (see kuramoto_model()), over the \(k\)-simplices.

  • theta – Initial phases, None for uniform ones (see kuramoto_model()).

  • component – Index of the component (0 is the largest).

  • runs – Number of independent runs.

  • adiabatic – Whether to follow the values of sigma in order (see kuramoto_model()).

  • time – Integration time of each value of sigma.

  • dt – Time step.

  • transient – Time discarded before averaging, or None for half of time.

  • seed – Seed of the random numbers, or None for a random seed.

  • phases – Whether to also return the final phases.

  • progress – Whether to show a progress bar over the time steps, or None for the global setting (see Progress bars).

Returns:

pandas.DataFrame with one row per value of sigma and run: columns sigma, run, order, order_down and order_up; with phases, also the final phases, a tensor [n_k, len(sigma), runs].

Raises:

ValueError – If net is a hypergraph, k is out of range, explosive lacks the simplices of one projection, a vector has the wrong shape, or the times are inconsistent.

Example:

import graphoskop as gsk
import numpy as np

net = gsk.clique_promotion(gsk.load_network("edges.tsv"))       # the clique complex
up = np.linspace(0, 3, 31)
loop = gsk.simplicial_kuramoto_model(net, np.r_[up, up[::-1]], explosive = True, omega = (2., 1.), adiabatic = True, seed = 0)

Kuramoto model

graphoskop.kuramoto_model(net, sigma, omega=(0.0, 1.0), theta=None, component=0, weight=None, runs=1, adiabatic=False, time=100.0, dt=0.01, transient=None, seed=None, phases=False, progress=None)

Kuramoto model on the nodes of a network: every node is an oscillator with phase \(\theta_i\) and natural frequency \(\omega_i\), pulled by its neighbors,

\[\dot\theta_i = \omega_i + \sigma \sum_j A_{ji} \sin(\theta_j - \theta_i),\]

with \(A_{ji}\) the weight of the edge from \(j\) to \(i\) (directed networks: a node follows the nodes with edges towards it). For undirected networks this is \(\dot\theta = \omega - \sigma B_1 W \sin(B_1^T \theta)\). The coupling is not divided by the degree or the number of nodes: scale sigma for that. The order parameter order is \(R = |\frac{1}{n} \sum_j e^{i \theta_j}|\), averaged over time after the transient. On a simplicial complex, the model runs on its 1-skeleton (for oscillators on its edges, see simplicial_kuramoto_model()). See the module description for sigma, runs and adiabatic.

Simplicial complexes: supported; hypergraphs: not supported (see Higher-order networks).

Parameters:

  • net – A Network or a simplicial complex.

  • sigma – Coupling strength, or sequence of them.

  • omega – Natural frequencies: (mean, sd) to draw them from a normal distribution for every run, or a vector over the nodes of the component (shared by the runs), or a matrix with one column per run.

  • theta – Initial phases, as omega but without (mean, sd); None to draw them uniformly in \([0, 2 \pi)\) for every run.

  • component – Index of the component (0 is the largest).

  • weight – Name of the edge attribute used as edge weight, or None for unit weights.

  • runs – Number of independent runs (frequencies and initial phases).

  • adiabatic – Whether to follow the values of sigma in order, each from the final phases of the previous one (hysteresis), instead of all at once from the initial phases.

  • time – Integration time of each value of sigma.

  • dt – Time step.

  • transient – Time discarded before averaging the order parameter, or None for half of time.

  • seed – Seed of the random numbers, or None for a random seed.

  • phases – Whether to also return the final phases.

  • progress – Whether to show a progress bar over the time steps, or None for the global setting (see Progress bars).

Returns:

pandas.DataFrame with one row per value of sigma (in the given order) and run: columns sigma, run and order; with phases, also the final phases, a tensor [n, len(sigma), runs].

Raises:

ValueError – If net is a hypergraph, a vector has the wrong shape, or the times are inconsistent.

Example:

import graphoskop as gsk
import numpy as np

net = gsk.load_network("edges.tsv")
curve = gsk.kuramoto_model(net, np.linspace(0, 1, 21), runs = 10, seed = 0)
curve.groupby("sigma")["order"].mean()                 # the synchronization transition
up = np.linspace(0, 1, 21)
loop = gsk.kuramoto_model(net, np.r_[up, up[::-1]], adiabatic = True, seed = 0)
