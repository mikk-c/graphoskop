Equivalence

Equivalence of nodes: how far two nodes have the same position in the network.

Two nodes are structurally equivalent (Lorrain and White, 1971) when they have exactly the same neighbors; structural_equivalence() measures how close two nodes are to it. Two nodes are automorphically equivalent when some relabeling of the nodes keeping all edges (an automorphism) maps one onto the other: they cannot be told apart by the structure of the network, although their neighbors can differ. Structurally equivalent nodes are automorphically equivalent; automorphic_equivalence() measures how close two nodes are to it. Two nodes are regularly equivalent when they are joined to equivalent nodes, not necessarily the same ones; regular_equivalence() measures how close two nodes are to it with the classical REGE algorithm, and path_regular_equivalence() with the path counts of Leicht, Holme and Newman (2006), a different quantity that mostly reflects proximity. Structural equivalence implies automorphic equivalence, which implies regular equivalence. The pairwise_ versions give the scores of all pairs of nodes at once, as n by n matrices, much faster than calling the single versions on every pair.

graphoskop.automorphic_equivalence(net, u, v, seed=0)

Degree of automorphic equivalence of two nodes (Borgatti and Everett, 1992): 1 if some relabeling of the nodes that keeps all edges (an automorphism) maps one onto the other, so that they have the same position in the structure of the network; less the more their positions differ.

As in the role similarity of Everett and Borgatti (1988), nodes are compared by their profiles of geodesic distances: the number of nodes at each distance from the node (pairs of distances from and to it in directed networks; unreachable nodes, also in other components, count at distance infinity). An automorphism keeps distances, so automorphically equivalent nodes have the same profile. The score is the weighted Jaccard coefficient of the two profiles, \(\sum_k \min(P_{uk}, P_{vk}) / \sum_k \max(P_{uk}, P_{vk})\) (Everett and Borgatti compare them by Euclidean distance; this puts the comparison between 0 and 1). When the profiles are equal, an automorphism mapping u onto v is searched exactly, by individualization and refinement (the strategy of nauty) on two copies of the network, one with u marked and one with v: if it exists the score is 1, otherwise (equal distance profiles without equivalence) it is \(1 - 1 / n\), with \(n\) the number of nodes of the network, so that 1 always means automorphic equivalence.

The automorphisms considered keep edge directions, self loops and, in multilayer networks, the layer of every copy; weights and edge multiplicities are ignored. The whole network is used (automorphisms can swap isomorphic components). The profiles take two breadth first searches; the search for an automorphism is usually immediate, but can take exponential time on rare, very symmetric networks. It runs on the CPU. A node is equivalent to itself (score 1).

Hypergraphs and simplicial complexes: not supported (see Higher-order networks).

Parameters:

  • net – A Network.

  • u – A node (input id or internal id (component, index); in multilayer networks a copy (entity, layer)).

  • v – Another node.

  • seed – Seed of the random hashes of refinement.

Returns:

The score, a float between 0 and 1.

Raises:

  • ValueError – If a node stands for several copies.

  • KeyError – If there is no such node.

Example:

import graphoskop as gsk

net = gsk.load_network("edges.tsv")
gsk.automorphic_equivalence(net, "alice", "bob")
graphoskop.pairwise_automorphic_equivalence(net, component=0, seed=0, batch_size=16777216)

Degree of automorphic equivalence (see automorphic_equivalence()) of all pairs of nodes.

The distance profiles of all nodes come from one breadth first search per node (on the CPU). Every profile counts all \(N\) nodes of the network, so the weighted Jaccard coefficient of two profiles is \(M / (2N - M)\), with \(M\) the sum of their minima, computed for all pairs in blocks of rows (about batch_size numbers at once) on the device of the network. Pairs with equal profiles are then split into automorphism orbits: twins (nodes with the same neighbors) are equivalent and merged first, the other nodes are separated by color refinement, which automorphisms keep, and each automorphism found by the exact search is applied to all nodes at once (it maps every node to an equivalent one), so most pairs are settled without a search of their own. Pairs in the same orbit score 1, the others with equal profiles \(1 - 1 / N\).

With a component index, its nodes are compared (the nodes of other components count as unreachable in the profiles, as in automorphic_equivalence()); component = None compares all nodes of the network, so that isomorphic components are found equivalent.

Hypergraphs and simplicial complexes: not supported (see Higher-order networks).

Parameters:

  • net – A Network.

  • component – Index of the component (0 is the largest), or None for the whole network.

  • seed – Seed of the random hashes of refinement.

  • batch_size – Bound on the numbers held at once while comparing profiles.

Returns:

Dense n by n float64 tensor on the device of the network, rows and columns ordered by internal index (component by component with None).

Example:

import graphoskop as gsk

net = gsk.load_network("edges.tsv")
A = gsk.pairwise_automorphic_equivalence(net)
orbits = (A == 1)                          # automorphic equivalence, as a boolean matrix
graphoskop.pairwise_path_regular_equivalence(net, component=0, alpha=0.97, weight=None)

Path-based regular equivalence of all pairs of nodes: the vertex similarity of Leicht, Holme and Newman (2006).

Two nodes are regularly equivalent (White and Reitz, 1983) when they are joined to equivalent nodes, not necessarily the same ones (as structural equivalence requires). Leicht, Holme and Newman make it graded and recursive in a different way from regular_equivalence(): \(i\) is similar to \(j\) if \(i\) has a neighbor similar to \(j\), \(S = \phi A S + I\) (every node is similar to itself), so \(S = (I - \phi A)^{-1} = \sum_l \phi^l A^l\), which counts the paths of every length \(l\) between the two nodes. Each count is divided by its expected value in a random network with the same degrees, \(k_i k_j \lambda_1^{l - 1} / 2m\), with \(\lambda_1\) the largest eigenvalue of \(A\), and longer paths are discounted by \(\alpha^l\):

\[S = 2 m \lambda_1 D^{-1} \left( I - \frac{\alpha}{\lambda_1} A \right)^{-1} D^{-1},\]

with \(D\) the diagonal matrix of the degrees (strengths with weight) and \(m\) the number (total weight) of edges. \(\alpha\) must be between 0 and 1; values close to 1 (0.9 to 0.99, as recommended by the authors) give weight to the whole structure of the network, small values reduce the measure to common neighbors over \(k_i k_j\), a structural equivalence (the \(l = 2\) term). Similarities are ratios of observed to expected paths, non negative and not bounded by 1; they rank pairs within a network.

Since it counts paths between the two nodes, this similarity rewards proximity: nodes with the same role far apart score low. E.g. in two identical families (a parent with four children) whose parents are joined by a path of six edges, the two parents (regularly, and even automorphically, equivalent) score 5.4 with \(\alpha = 0.97\), while a parent and one of its own children score 68.7; over all pairs, similarities follow the shortest path distance closely (Spearman correlation 0.87 to 0.97 for \(\alpha \leq 0.5\), 0.25 to 0.75 for \(\alpha = 0.97\), on Zachary’s karate club, a Barabasi-Albert and a Watts-Strogatz network). For roles independent of distance see regular_equivalence() (the classical measure) and automorphic_equivalence().

Edges are taken as undirected (the expected path counts assume it) and weights as their multiplicities; a self loop adds its weight once to the diagonal of \(A\). Each component is computed on its own (pairs in different components have no paths); nodes without edges get 0. The inverse is a dense n by n matrix, computed by Cholesky factorization (\(I - \frac{\alpha}{\lambda_1} A\) is positive definite) on the device of the network.

Hypergraphs and simplicial complexes: not supported (see Higher-order networks).

Parameters:

  • net – A Network.

  • component – Index of the component (0 is the largest).

  • alpha – Weight of longer paths, between 0 and 1 (0.9 to 0.99 recommended).

  • weight – Name of the edge attribute used as weight, or None for unit weights.

Returns:

Dense n by n float64 tensor on the device of the network, rows and columns ordered by internal index.

Raises:

ValueError – If alpha is not between 0 and 1 or weights are negative.

Example:

import graphoskop as gsk

net = gsk.load_network("edges.tsv")
S = gsk.pairwise_path_regular_equivalence(net, alpha = 0.95)
graphoskop.pairwise_regular_equivalence(net, component=0, weight=None, iterations=3, tol=1e-10, batch_size=16777216)

Degree of regular equivalence of all pairs of nodes, by REGE (White and Reitz, 1983), in the form given by Wasserman and Faust (1994, section 12.4).

Two nodes are regularly equivalent when they are joined to equivalent nodes, not necessarily the same ones: two parents are equivalent because both have children, whoever the children are. REGE starts with all pairs equivalent (\(M^0_{ij} = 1\)) and, at every iteration, matches each neighbor \(k\) of \(i\) to the neighbor \(m\) of \(j\) most equivalent to it, with ties in the same directions and of similar values:

\[M^{t+1}_{ij} = \frac{\sum_k \max_m M^t_{km} \left( \min(x_{ik}, x_{jm}) + \min(x_{ki}, x_{mj}) \right) + (i \leftrightarrow j)} {\sum_k \left( \max(x_{ik}, x_{jm^*}) + \max(x_{ki}, x_{m^*j}) \right) + (i \leftrightarrow j)},\]

with \(k\) over the neighbors of \(i\) (in either direction), \(m\) over those of \(j\), \(m^*\) the best match (ties broken by the smallest denominator; a node without neighbors offers no match), \(x\) the edge values (weight, or 1; parallel edges summed), and \((i \leftrightarrow j)\) the same sums matching the neighbors of \(j\) to those of \(i\). Scores are between 0 and 1, 1 for regularly equivalent nodes, and do not increase from one iteration to the next. UCINET runs 3 iterations by default, as here, which gives graded scores; with iterations = None they are run until the scores change by less than tol, which tends to separate the regularly equivalent pairs (1) from the others (towards 0). Two nodes without neighbors score 1 (they are regularly equivalent), a node with neighbors and one without score 0.

Regular equivalence needs directions or values to tell nodes apart: in an undirected network with unit values, every node with neighbors is regularly equivalent to every other (each of its neighbors matches any neighbor of the other, all equivalent at the start), so all such pairs score 1. Use it on directed or valued networks; for undirected unweighted ones see automorphic_equivalence(). Unlike path_regular_equivalence(), it does not depend on the distance between the two nodes: nodes in different components can be equivalent, so component = None compares all nodes of the network. E.g. with two separate families, a parent with edges to four children and one with edges to three, the two parents score 1, and so do any two children; a parent and a child score 0. The parents are not automorphically equivalent (the families differ in size), and have no paths between them.

Every iteration compares every neighbor relation \((i, k)\) with every relation \((j, m)\), \((2m)^2\) comparisons for \(m\) edges, in blocks of about batch_size numbers on the device of the network.

Hypergraphs and simplicial complexes: not supported (see Higher-order networks).

Parameters:

  • net – A Network.

  • component – Index of the component (0 is the largest), or None for the whole network.

  • weight – Name of the edge attribute used as edge value, or None for unit values.

  • iterations – Number of iterations (3 as UCINET), or None to iterate until the scores change by less than tol.

  • tol – Tolerance, with iterations = None.

  • batch_size – Bound on the numbers held at once.

Returns:

Dense n by n float64 tensor on the device of the network, rows and columns ordered by internal index (component by component with None).

Raises:

ValueError – If weights are negative.

Example:

import graphoskop as gsk

net = gsk.load_network("edges.tsv", directed = True)
R = gsk.pairwise_regular_equivalence(net, component = None)
R = gsk.pairwise_regular_equivalence(net, weight = "weight", iterations = None)
graphoskop.pairwise_structural_equivalence(net, component=0, mode='both', weight=None, batch_size=16777216)

Degree of structural equivalence (see structural_equivalence()) of all pairs of nodes.

The overlaps \(\sum_x \min(w_{ux}, w_{vx})\) of all pairs are accumulated over the common neighbors: every node \(x\) contributes to each pair of its neighbors, so the work is \(\sum_x d_x^2\) (the number of 2-paths), done in blocks of about batch_size pairs on the device of the network; then \(J_{uv} = M_{uv} / (s_u + s_v - M_{uv})\), with \(s\) the number (or total weight) of neighbors. Pairs of nodes in different components score 0, so a component is computed on its own; component = None gives the matrix of the whole network.

Hypergraphs and simplicial complexes: not supported (see Higher-order networks).

Parameters:

  • net – A Network.

  • component – Index of the component (0 is the largest), or None for the whole network.

  • mode – "both", "out" or "in"; ignored in undirected networks.

  • weight – Name of the edge attribute used as weight, or None for the plain Jaccard coefficient.

  • batch_size – Largest number of neighbor pairs expanded at once.

Returns:

Dense n by n float64 tensor on the device of the network, rows and columns ordered by internal index (component by component with None).

Raises:

ValueError – If mode is not valid or weights are negative.

Example:

import graphoskop as gsk

net = gsk.load_network("edges.tsv")
J = gsk.pairwise_structural_equivalence(net)
c, i = net.node_map["alice"]
c, j = net.node_map["bob"]
J[i, j]                                 # if both are in component 0
graphoskop.path_regular_equivalence(net, u, v, alpha=0.97, weight=None, tol=1e-12, max_iter=100000)

Path-based regular equivalence of two nodes: the vertex similarity of Leicht, Holme and Newman (2006), where a node is similar to another if its neighbors are similar to it, counting the paths between them. See pairwise_path_regular_equivalence() for the definition and how it differs from regular_equivalence(); this computes a single pair by iterating \(x = \frac{\alpha}{\lambda_1} A x + e_v\) on the device of the network (no n by n matrix), until the vector changes by less than tol per node.

Hypergraphs and simplicial complexes: not supported (see Higher-order networks).

Parameters:

  • net – A Network.

  • u – A node (input id or internal id (component, index); in multilayer networks a copy (entity, layer)).

  • v – Another node.

  • alpha – Weight of longer paths, between 0 and 1 (0.9 to 0.99 recommended).

  • weight – Name of the edge attribute used as weight, or None for unit weights.

  • tol – Tolerance per node.

  • max_iter – Largest number of iterations.

Returns:

The similarity, a non negative float (not bounded by 1).

Raises:

  • ValueError – If alpha is not between 0 and 1, weights are negative, or a node stands for several copies.

  • KeyError – If there is no such node.

Example:

import graphoskop as gsk

net = gsk.load_network("edges.tsv")
gsk.path_regular_equivalence(net, "alice", "bob")
graphoskop.regular_equivalence(net, u, v, weight=None, iterations=3, tol=1e-10, batch_size=16777216)

Degree of regular equivalence of two nodes, by REGE (White and Reitz, 1983): two nodes are regularly equivalent when they are joined to equivalent nodes, not necessarily the same ones. See pairwise_regular_equivalence() for the algorithm. The scores of a pair depend on those of the pairs of their neighbors, and so on, so all pairs of nodes of the components of u and v are computed, and the pair is picked.

Hypergraphs and simplicial complexes: not supported (see Higher-order networks).

Parameters:

  • net – A Network.

  • u – A node (input id or internal id (component, index); in multilayer networks a copy (entity, layer)).

  • v – Another node.

  • weight – Name of the edge attribute used as edge value, or None for unit values.

  • iterations – Number of iterations (3 as UCINET), or None to iterate until the scores change by less than tol.

  • tol – Tolerance, with iterations = None.

  • batch_size – Bound on the numbers held at once.

Returns:

The score, a float between 0 and 1.

Raises:

  • ValueError – If weights are negative, or a node stands for several copies.

  • KeyError – If there is no such node.

Example:

import graphoskop as gsk

net = gsk.load_network("edges.tsv", directed = True)
gsk.regular_equivalence(net, "alice", "bob")
graphoskop.structural_equivalence(net, u, v, mode='both', weight=None)

Degree of structural equivalence of two nodes: the Jaccard coefficient of their sets of neighbors, \(|N(u) \cap N(v)| / |N(u) \cup N(v)|\), 1 if they have the same neighbors and 0 if they have none in common.

In directed networks, mode chooses the neighbors: "out" (successors), "in" (predecessors) or "both", where a node is equivalent only if it has the same successors and the same predecessors (the two sets are compared together: a successor of one and a predecessor of the other do not match). With weight, the weighted Jaccard (Ruzicka) coefficient \(\sum_x \min(w_{ux}, w_{vx}) / \sum_x \max(w_{ux}, w_{vx})\), with the weights of the edges to each neighbor (summed over parallel edges); weights must be non negative.

Neighbors are taken as they are: a self loop makes a node its own neighbor, and two adjacent nodes count each other as neighbors (so the two nodes of an isolated edge score 0, although they are interchangeable). Two nodes without neighbors score 0 (as NetworkX’s jaccard_coefficient). Nodes in different components share no neighbors. In multilayer networks neighbors are those of the supra-graph: copies in different layers are different neighbors, and the copies of a node in other layers, joined by couplings, are neighbors too. An entity stands for all its copies, with the union of their neighbors.

Hypergraphs and simplicial complexes: not supported (see Higher-order networks).

Parameters:

  • net – A Network.

  • u – A node (input id, internal id (component, index), or entity in multilayer networks).

  • v – Another node.

  • mode – "both", "out" or "in"; ignored in undirected networks.

  • weight – Name of the edge attribute used as weight, or None for the plain Jaccard coefficient.

Returns:

The score, a float between 0 and 1.

Raises:

  • ValueError – If mode is not valid or weights are negative.

  • KeyError – If there is no such node.

Example:

import graphoskop as gsk

net = gsk.load_network("edges.tsv")
gsk.structural_equivalence(net, "alice", "bob")
gsk.structural_equivalence(net, "alice", "bob", weight = "weight")
