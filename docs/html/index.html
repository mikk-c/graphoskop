Graphoskop

Graphoskop: analysis of networks and of vectors defined on their nodes.

All public functions and classes are available at the top level of the package.

Quick start, with an edge list edges.tsv:

source   target   weight
alice    bob      1.5
bob      carol    2.0

and a node table nodes.tsv:

id       age   height   city
alice    34    170      Rome
carol    29    165      Oslo

import graphoskop as gsk

net = gsk.load_network("edges.tsv", "nodes.tsv")
g = net.components[0]      # largest weakly connected component
g.edge_index               # edges, internal node ids
g.edge_attr                # weight, one row per edge
g.node_attr                # age and height (numeric), one row per node
net.qualitative_node_attr  # city (qualitative), always in CPU memory
net.node_map["alice"]      # (component, index)

Multilayer networks are loaded with multilayer = True from an edge list with a layer column; nodes are then copies (entity, layer) (see Multilayer networks). Hypergraphs and simplicial complexes, interactions among any number of nodes, are loaded with load_hypergraph() and load_simplicial_complex() and stored as incidence graphs; functions say whether they support them (see Higher-order networks). Higher-order dependencies in paths (where one goes next depends on where one came from) are kept in an ordinary directed network by higher_order_dependency_network() and memory_network(). Signed networks carry a +1 / -1 sign on their edges, built from a positive and a negative layer or by thresholding a weight (see Signed networks).

Synthetic networks come from generators, which can also randomize an existing network, keeping its nodes and their attributes, or, with neural generators such as GraphRNN, learn to produce networks like observed ones (see Generators).

Exponential random graph models, with node attributes and dyadic covariates, are written and fitted as in R’s ergm package (see Exponential random graph models).

Epidemics spread on networks with compartmental models (SI, SIS, SIR), by simple or complex contagion, simulated in parallel runs (see Epidemics). The robustness of a network to node or edge failures and attacks, another percolation process, and cascades of failures driven by loads and capacities are in Robustness. Oscillators coupled on the nodes of a network, or on the edges and triangles of a simplicial complex, synchronize with the Kuramoto model and its higher-order versions (see Synchronization).

Core-periphery structures, a dense core of nodes and a sparse periphery attached to it, are fit with the discrete and continuous models of Borgatti and Everett and the model of Rombach et al. (see Core-periphery structure).

Isomorphism tests tell whether two networks are the same up to a relabeling of their nodes, and whether a network contains another one, exactly (a compiled parallel search) or approximately (Weisfeiler-Leman color refinement and candidate filtering on tensors; see Isomorphism).

Frequent subgraph mining finds the labeled patterns that occur in many components of a network, or many times in a single network (minimum image based support), with gSpan, and the association rules between patterns that differ by one edge (see Frequent subgraph mining).

Graph neural networks predict a node attribute from the others and the network, with the layers of PyTorch Geometric (GCN, GraphSAGE, GAT and GATv2), in one call that prepares the features, splits the nodes, trains and scores; graph autoencoders (GAE and VGAE) learn node vectors from the attributes and the edges without a target; node2vec, Laplacian eigenmaps and locally linear embeddings give every node a vector from the structure alone, struc2vec one that reflects its structural role, metapath2vec one from walks along chains of node types, and DiffPool every graph (see Graph neural networks).

Link prediction hides some edges of a network and scores the hidden pairs, and as many unconnected ones, from the rest of the network (see Link prediction). Network error correction does both jobs of cleaning observed networks, finding spurious and missing edges, with the nested stochastic blockmodel (see Network error correction).

Layouts place the nodes in the plane or in space to draw the network, storing the positions as node attributes pos_x, pos_y (and pos_z); the circular and grid layouts make sectors and blocks of groups (nested for hierarchical groups), and the matrix layout orders the rows and columns of adjacency matrices (independently in bipartite networks); the hierarchical layout draws layers (directed acyclic networks, trees), top-down or radially; the force-directed layout keeps groups of densely connected nodes together; UMAP lays out node attributes, node embeddings or the adjacency matrix; any layout can be mirrored, rotated and zoomed, and its overlaps removed, also for nodes of different sizes, and its edges bundled along a hierarchy of groups (see Layouts).

Long computations show a progress bar with the time left, at no measurable cost (see Progress bars).
