Exponential random graph models

Exponential random graph models (ERGMs): the probability of a network \(y\) is \(P_\theta(y) = e^{\theta^T g(y)} / \kappa(\theta)\), where \(g(y)\) is a vector of network statistics (number of edges, of triangles, of edges between nodes with the same value of an attribute, …) and \(\theta\) their coefficients: a positive coefficient makes networks with more of that statistic more likely, all else equal. Equivalently, the log-odds of a tie, given the rest of the network, is \(\theta^T \delta_{ij}(y)\), where \(\delta_{ij}(y)\) is the change in the statistics when the tie is added (Robins, Pattison, Kalish and Lusher, 2007).

Models are written as in R’s ergm package (Hunter, Handcock, Butts, Goodreau and Morris, 2008), with the same terms, statistics and coefficient names, so that results can be compared:

fit = gsk.ergm(net, "edges + nodematch('city') + nodecov('age') + gwesp(0.5, fixed = TRUE)")

Node attributes enter through terms such as nodecov (numeric), nodefactor (one dummy variable per category of a qualitative attribute, the first category being the baseline), nodematch (homophily) and nodemix (mixing between categories); dyadic covariates, such as the weights of another network, through edgecov. See ergm() for the list of terms.

Markov chain Monte Carlo on networks is sequential (every step depends on the network left by the previous one), so it does not benefit from a GPU: change statistics and the sampler are compiled with Numba and run on the CPU, a few chains in parallel threads, on the dense adjacency matrix. Models are meant for networks of up to a few thousand nodes, as ERGMs usually are. The first fit of a session compiles the kernels (cached on disk afterwards). Multilayer and bipartite networks are not supported yet.

import graphoskop as gsk

net = gsk.load_network("edges.tsv", "nodes.tsv")                  # node attributes age (numeric), city (qualitative)
gsk.ergm_statistics(net, "edges + triangle + nodematch('city')")  # g(y), as R's summary(net ~ ...)

fit = gsk.ergm(net, "edges + nodecov('age') + nodefactor('city') + nodematch('city')")   # dyad independent: exact MLE
fit.summary                                                       # estimates, standard errors, z, p
fit.aic

fit = gsk.ergm(net, "edges + nodematch('city') + gwesp(0.5, fixed = TRUE)", seed = 1)   # MCMC MLE
fit.coef, fit.stderr, fit.converged
fit.samples.mean() - fit.observed                                 # sampled statistics around the observed ones

Fitting

graphoskop.ergm(net, formula, method='mcmc', covariates=None, samplesize=1024, burnin=16384, interval=1024, max_iterations=20, tolerance=0.1, chains=None, seed=None)

Fit an exponential random graph model (ERGM), as R’s ergm().

The model gives every network \(y\) the probability \(P_\theta(y) = e^{\theta^T g(y)} / \kappa(\theta)\), with \(g\) the statistics of the terms of formula (see below). Estimation follows R’s ergm:

  • the maximum pseudo-likelihood estimate (MPLE; Strauss and Ikeda, 1990): a logistic regression of every dyad on its change statistics, computed on the distinct rows of change statistics. For dyad independent models (only terms whose change statistics do not depend on the rest of the network, such as edges, nodecov, nodematch, edgecov) it is the maximum likelihood estimate, with exact standard errors and log-likelihood;

  • otherwise, the Monte Carlo maximum likelihood estimate (MCMC MLE; Geyer and Thompson, 1992), starting from the MPLE: networks are sampled at the current coefficients by Markov chain Monte Carlo, the log-likelihood ratio is approximated from their statistics and maximized, and the step repeats until the observed statistics are well within those sampled. When the observed statistics are outside the convex hull of the sample, the step aims at a point between them and the sample mean (Hummel, Hunter and Handcock, 2012); steps are also kept short enough for the sample to approximate the likelihood (effective sample size of the importance weights at least 5%). Standard errors come from the inverse information matrix (covariance of statistics sampled at the estimate) plus the Monte Carlo error (from batch means), whose share is reported as MCMC %.

Networks are sampled by Metropolis-Hastings with R’s tie / no tie proposal, compiled, with chains chains in parallel threads, each started at the observed network and continued from one iteration to the next: in every iteration each chain makes burnin steps, then samplesize / chains samples, interval steps apart (R’s defaults). Convergence: the coefficients are updated until the observed statistics are within tolerance standard deviations of the mean sampled ones (Mahalanobis distance, which is about the distance of the estimate from the MLE in standard errors; at least twice its Monte Carlo noise, \(2 \sqrt{p / samplesize}\)); a last sample at the estimate gives the standard errors. When the MPLE is infinite or very large (separation), the chains start from a ridge penalized MPLE; if the sample cannot move the estimate for two iterations, the coefficients go halfway back to a density only model. A model that only produces empty or complete networks (degenerate) triggers a warning.

Terms (as in R; attr is a node attribute name, numeric or qualitative, missing values matching nothing):

  • edges: number of ties;

  • nodecov(attr): sum over ties of the sum of the attribute of the two nodes (numeric); directed variants nodeicov, nodeocov (receiver, sender);

  • nodefactor(attr, levels = -1): per category, number of tie ends at nodes of that category, the first (in sorted order) being the baseline (levels = TRUE for all); directed nodeifactor, nodeofactor. This is how qualitative attributes enter as dummy variables;

  • nodematch(attr, diff = FALSE, levels = NULL): ties between nodes with the same value (homophily), one statistic, or one per category with diff = TRUE;

  • nodemix(attr, levels2 = -1): ties per pair of categories (ordered if directed), the first pair being the baseline;

  • absdiff(attr, pow = 1): sum over ties of \(|x_i - x_j|^{pow}\);

  • edgecov(x, attrname = NULL): sum over ties of a dyadic covariate covariates[x]: an n by n matrix (network wide node order, np.concatenate(net.node_ids)), a table of source, target, value rows, or another Network on the same nodes (its ties, or their edge attribute attrname). Edge attributes of the modelled network itself cannot be covariates: they exist only on ties, so they would predict ties perfectly; they are covariates when they come from another relation (e.g. a past wave, another layer);

  • mutual, asymmetric: reciprocated and unreciprocated dyads (directed);

  • triangle: triangles (directed: transitive plus cyclic triples), ttriple, ctriple (directed);

  • kstar(k): k-stars (undirected); istar(k), ostar(k) (directed); k can be a vector, e.g. kstar(2:3);

  • degree(d), idegree(d), odegree(d): nodes with degree d; concurrent: nodes with degree at least 2; isolates;

  • gwdegree(decay), gwidegree, gwodegree: geometrically weighted degree distribution, \(e^\alpha \sum_k (1 - (1 - e^{-\alpha})^k) D_k\);

  • gwesp(decay), gwdsp(decay): geometrically weighted edgewise and dyadwise shared partners (directed: outgoing two-paths, R’s default OTP); esp(d), dsp(d): ties and dyads with exactly d shared partners.

The decay of geometrically weighted terms is fixed: in R, write fixed = TRUE for the same model (R’s default fixed = FALSE estimates it as a curved ERGM, not supported here). Self loops are left out, parallel edges count once, and the network is the union of its components.

Parameters:

  • net – A Network, neither multilayer nor bipartite.

  • formula – Model formula, as in R, with or without net ~: "edges + nodematch('city', diff = TRUE) + gwesp(0.5, fixed = TRUE)".

  • method – "mcmc" (MCMC MLE, or exact MLE for dyad independent models) or "mple".

  • covariates – Dictionary of dyadic covariates for edgecov terms, or None.

  • samplesize – Number of networks sampled per iteration.

  • burnin – Steps of every chain before sampling, in every iteration.

  • interval – Steps of every chain between samples.

  • max_iterations – Maximum number of MCMC MLE iterations.

  • tolerance – Convergence tolerance, in standard deviations of the statistics.

  • chains – Number of chains, run in parallel threads; None for the number of Numba threads (by default, the number of CPU cores).

  • seed – Seed of the random numbers, or None for a random seed.

Returns:

ERGMFit.

Raises:

  • ValueError – For unknown terms or arguments, terms that do not apply to the network (e.g. mutual on an undirected one), attributes with several values for some node, or multilayer or bipartite networks.

  • KeyError – For missing node attributes or dyadic covariates.

Example:

import graphoskop as gsk

net = gsk.load_network("edges.tsv", "nodes.tsv")                    # node attributes age, city
fit = gsk.ergm(net, "edges + nodecov('age') + nodematch('city')")    # dyad independent: exact MLE
fit.summary
fit = gsk.ergm(net, "edges + nodematch('city') + gwesp(0.5, fixed = TRUE)", seed = 1)
fit.coef["gwesp.fixed.0.5"], fit.stderr["gwesp.fixed.0.5"]

trade = gsk.load_network("trade.tsv")                                # another relation, same nodes
fit = gsk.ergm(net, "edges + edgecov('trade', 'weight')", covariates = {"trade": trade})
class graphoskop.ERGMFit(names, coef, cov, mcmc, observed, mple, method, iterations, converged, samples, loglik, dyads)

A fitted exponential random graph model, as returned by ergm().

Variables:

  • coef – pandas.Series of estimated coefficients, by statistic.

  • stderr – pandas.Series of standard errors (including the Monte Carlo error).

  • summary – pandas.DataFrame with columns Estimate, Std. Error, MCMC % (share of the variance of the estimate due to the Monte Carlo sample), z value and Pr(>|z|), as R’s summary().

  • observed – pandas.Series of the statistics of the network.

  • mple – pandas.Series of the maximum pseudo-likelihood estimates.

  • method – "MLE" (exact, dyad independent models), "MCMC MLE" or "MPLE".

  • iterations – Number of Monte Carlo MLE iterations.

  • converged – Whether the Monte Carlo MLE converged.

  • samples – pandas.DataFrame of the statistics sampled at the estimate (MCMC MLE), or None.

  • loglik – Log-likelihood (dyad independent models; pseudo-likelihood for the MPLE of dependent ones), or None.

  • aic – Akaike information criterion, from loglik, or None.

  • bic – Bayesian information criterion, with the number of dyads as sample size, or None.

simulate(count=1, burnin=16384, interval=1024, seed=None)

Networks drawn from the fitted model, as R’s simulate(): Markov chains at the estimated coefficients, started at the observed network, give a network after burnin steps and then one every interval steps (several chains run in parallel threads). Simulated networks keep the nodes of the observed one, with their ids and attributes (see Generators); their edges take the attributes of observed edges, drawn at random.

Parameters:

  • count – Number of networks.

  • burnin – Steps of every chain before its first network.

  • interval – Steps of every chain between networks.

  • seed – Seed of the random numbers, or None for a random seed.

Returns:

List of Network.

Example:

fit = gsk.ergm(net, "edges + nodematch('city') + gwesp(0.5, fixed = TRUE)", seed = 1)
sims = fit.simulate(100, seed = 2)
[gsk.average_clustering(g) for g in sims]
gof(count=100, burnin=16384, interval=1024, seed=None)

Goodness of fit, as R’s gof(): distributions of statistics of the observed network compared with those of count networks simulated from the fitted model (see simulate()): the degree distribution (in- and out-degrees if directed), edgewise shared partners (outgoing two-paths if directed), geodesic distances (number of dyads at each distance, inf for unreachable ones) and the statistics of the model. For every value the table gives the observed count, the minimum, mean and maximum of the simulated ones and the Monte Carlo p-value, \(\min(1, 2 \min(P(sim \leq obs), P(sim \geq obs)))\): small values flag features of the network the model does not reproduce.

Parameters:

  • count – Number of simulated networks.

  • burnin – Steps of every chain before its first network.

  • interval – Steps of every chain between networks.

  • seed – Seed of the random numbers, or None.

Returns:

Dictionary from "model", "degree" ("idegree" and "odegree" if directed), "espartners" and "distance" to pandas.DataFrame with columns obs, min, mean, max and MC p-value.

pseudo_r2()

McFadden’s pseudo-\(R^2\) of the model, \(1 - \ell / \ell_0\), with \(\ell\) the log pseudo-likelihood of the model (logistic regression of the dyads on their change statistics; the log-likelihood for dyad independent models) and \(\ell_0\) that of the density only model (edges), and its decomposition among the statistics other than edges:

  • pmvd: proportional marginal variance decomposition (Feldman, 2005), as in R’s relaimpo: the average over all orders of the statistics of the increase of \(R^2\) when each is added, each order weighted proportionally to \(\prod_{i < p} 1 / (R^2 - R^2_{(i)})\), \(R^2_{(i)}\) that of the first i statistics of the order: a statistic that adds nothing given the others gets no share;

  • lmg: the same with equal weights (Lindeman, Merenda and Gold, 1980; relaimpo’s default).

Both add up to the pseudo-\(R^2\). They need the model refitted on all \(2^p\) subsets of the p statistics, computed once over subsets rather than over the \(p!\) orders, so they are given for up to 16 statistics.

Returns:

(r2, table): the pseudo-\(R^2\) and a pandas.DataFrame with columns pmvd and lmg, by statistic.

Raises:

ValueError – If there are more than 16 statistics besides edges.

Example:

r2, parts = fit.pseudo_r2()
parts["pmvd"] / r2                                    # shares of the explained pseudo-likelihood

Simulation and goodness of fit

sims = fit.simulate(100, seed = 2)                                  # networks from the fitted model, with the nodes of net
gof = fit.gof(100, seed = 3)                                        # as R's gof(): model, degree, espartners, distance
gof["degree"]                                                       # obs, min, mean, max, MC p-value per degree
r2, parts = fit.pseudo_r2()                                         # McFadden pseudo-R2 and its PMVD / LMG decomposition
gsk.randomization_test(net, gsk.average_clustering, fit, n = 100)   # clustering of net against the model

Statistics

graphoskop.ergm_statistics(net, formula, covariates=None)

Network statistics of an ERGM formula: the values of the statistics \(g(y)\) of the network, as R’s summary(net ~ ...).

Parameters:

  • net – A Network, neither multilayer nor bipartite.

  • formula – Model formula (see ergm()).

  • covariates – Dyadic covariates for edgecov terms (see ergm()).

Returns:

pandas.Series of statistics, by name.

Raises:

ValueError – See ergm().

Example:

import graphoskop as gsk

net = gsk.load_network("edges.tsv", "nodes.tsv")
gsk.ergm_statistics(net, "edges + triangle + nodematch('city')")
